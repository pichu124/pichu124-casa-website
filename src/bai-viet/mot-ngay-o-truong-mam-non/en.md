When your child goes to school all day, the question parents ask most is: what does my child do in class from morning to afternoon? This article walks through a day at Casa, from arrival, meals, learning and play and nap time to going home, with a few suggestions for supporting your child at home.

## Arrival and pick-up

Children attend from 7:30 to 17:30, Monday to Friday. Parents who need a later pick-up can register for late care until 18:30 (fees apply). Children keep coming to school in summer, with just 1 week off according to the school calendar, so families do not have to find childcare for a long holiday.

The school works with parents to keep information about who is picking up, drop-off and pick-up times and any changes up to date. If someone else is collecting your child today, let the teacher know in advance so pick-up is safe and smooth.

## Meals during the day

The school's own one-way kitchen cooks every meal, including cakes and snacks. The number of meals differs by campus:

| Campus | Meals a day |
| --- | --- |
| Kim Mã | 4: breakfast, morning snack, lunch, afternoon snack |
| Chùa Láng, Mỹ Đình | 5: breakfast, morning snack, lunch, afternoon snack and fresh milk at 16:00 |

At mealtimes, teachers stay with the children and help them learn to serve themselves and keep clean. Information about allergies or special dietary needs is taken on board so the school and parents can work out suitable care together.

## Learning time and play time

At preschool age, learning and play go hand in hand. Each age learns in its own way:

- **Nursery, 15–36 months:** classes guided by Montessori principles, focusing on independence, the senses, movement, language and practical life skills. Children get used to English through songs, games, stories and everyday conversation.
- **Kindergarten, 3–6 years:** a programme based on the Cambridge Early Years framework for children aged 3–6, learning through play across six areas of development. Depending on the campus, children follow the international track (a foreign teacher in every class) or the bilingual track (a foreign teacher teaches set lessons).
- **Children aged 5–6:** also follow the pre-primary programme to prepare for Grade 1. Chùa Láng and Mỹ Đình have a separate pre-primary class; at Kim Mã, it takes place in the afternoons.

Classrooms, activity rooms and play areas are set up for children to explore, move and practise skills, always with teachers observing and guiding.

To see a real day in class, you can watch the video "A day at the Kim Mã campus" on the [Kim Mã campus](/en/campuses/kim-ma/) page.

## Nap time

After lunch, children rest. Sleep gives them energy for the afternoon. New children may need a few days to get used to sleeping at school.

You can help by keeping weekend nap times close to school times, and by telling the teacher about your child's sleep habits at home: whether they like to lie on their side or hold something, whether they tend to wake early. Small details like these make it easier for the teacher to settle your child.

## How can you follow your child's day?

The school's communication app lets parents follow activities, menus and announcements and talk with teachers. Parents can also view live camera images at the school under the school's rules, managed by the school and respecting each child's privacy.

At the end of the day, a few minutes' chat with the teacher at pick-up also tells you a lot: whether your child ate well, slept well, and whether anything made them happy or upset.

## What can you do at home?

- **Keep a steady routine**, even at weekends: wake-up, meal and sleep times close to school times.
- **Ask your child specific questions**: "What did you eat today?", "Who did you play with?" rather than "Did you have fun at school?".
- **Put your child to bed early** after a long day at school, especially in the first weeks.
- **Tell the teacher** when something changes at home: your child slept badly, has just recovered from illness, or there is a new baby. The better the teacher knows your child, the better they can care for them.
