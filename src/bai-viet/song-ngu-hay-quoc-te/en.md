Many preschools in Hanoi offer two tracks: bilingual and international. The names sound the same from school to school, but what they mean differs. So rather than choosing by name, ask specifically: who is in the class, and from whom, and for how long, does your child hear English each day?

## How do the two tracks differ?

The biggest difference is usually the team in the class. In the international track, a foreign teacher is in the class every day, so children hear and use English during routines as well as lessons. In the bilingual track, Vietnamese is the main language of the class, a foreign teacher teaches set lessons, and children get to know English step by step.

At Casa, both tracks follow a programme based on the Cambridge Early Years framework for children aged 3–6. The tracks differ in who is in the class:

| In each class for ages 3–6 | International track | Bilingual track |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Homeroom teacher | 1 | 1 |
| Teaching assistants | 1 (preferably a strong English speaker) | 2 (at least 1 strong English speaker) |
| Foreign teacher | 1, in the class | Teaches set lessons |
| Campuses with this track | Chùa Láng, Mỹ Đình | Kim Mã |

## Five things to weigh

**1. What language does your child hear at home?** If the whole family speaks Vietnamese, the international track gives your child more time hearing English at school. The bilingual track keeps Vietnamese as the base and brings English in gradually. If your family already uses both languages, you can decide on the points below.

**2. Where will your child go to primary school?** If you already have a primary school in mind, choose a preschool track that leads on from that plan. Ask what the final kindergarten year does to prepare children for Grade 1.

**3. Does your child adapt quickly or need time?** Some children are happy with an English-speaking teacher straight away; others need a few weeks. Ask what teachers do when a child does not yet understand English, and whether children get support in Vietnamese when they need it.

**4. Is the campus close to home or work?** The school run affects the whole family every day, for years. At Casa, each campus has one track for ages 3–6: Kim Mã has the bilingual track; Chùa Láng and Mỹ Đình have the international track. All three campuses have nursery and pre-primary classes.

**5. Does the cost fit your family's plans?** A track with a foreign teacher in the class usually has a different fee schedule from the bilingual track. Ask for the fee schedule of each track and what each item covers, so you compare like with like.

## What to look at on your visit

If you can, ask to see a lesson with the foreign teacher. Notice how the teacher talks to the children, whether they respond eagerly, and how the assistant helps a child who has not understood. Ask too how the school tells parents about their child's progress in English. The full list is in the article [8 questions to ask when you visit a preschool](/en/news/8-questions-to-ask-on-a-preschool-visit/).

## Neither track is better for everyone

The right track is the one that fits your home language, your plans for school, your child's temperament and your family's routine. You can visit one bilingual campus and one international campus to feel the difference for yourself. See each track in detail on the [Programmes](/en/programmes/) page.
