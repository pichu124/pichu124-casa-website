For families living in the Ciputra urban area and Phú Thượng ward, or in nearby areas such as Xuân La and Starlake, choosing a preschool often starts with "which school is close to home". Being close matters, but it is not enough. This article suggests seven things to weigh so you can compare schools on the same criteria.

## 1. Distance and the school run

Work out the real journey at morning and afternoon rush hour, not just the distance on the map. Ask the school about drop-off and pick-up times, the latest pick-up time and whether late care is available. A school inside the urban area lets you walk or make a very short drive, which makes each morning easier for your child.

## 2. Which floor, and how safe

In urban areas, many preschools are inside buildings. Then you should ask carefully: which floor the classrooms are on, whether children go up by lift or stairs, where the emergency exits are, where drop-off takes place and who is allowed to pick children up.

## 3. Space to play and move

Preschool children need to run, jump and climb every day. Ask where children do active play, for how long each day, and where they play when it rains or is very hot.

## 4. A programme for each age

Children under 3 and children aged 3–6 need different ways of learning. Ask how classes are grouped by age and which programme each age follows. For young children, also ask how the school helps them become independent, move and start talking. For older children, ask about the final year that prepares them for Grade 1.

## 5. Tracks and English

If the school has more than one track, do not choose by name. Ask who is in the class, and whether the foreign teacher is in the class every day or teaches set lessons. The article [Bilingual or international: which track suits your child?](/en/news/bilingual-or-international-which-track-for-your-child/) helps you compare the two tracks.

## 6. Meals and the kitchen

Ask how many meals children have each day, how the menu changes, where the kitchen is and who cooks. If your child has allergies or is a fussy eater, ask how the school handles this at everyday meals.

## 7. When to register

New schools usually take early registrations before they open. It is worth looking a few months ahead so you have time to visit, compare and prepare your child.

## When you visit a school inside a building

Beyond the classrooms, walk the route your child will take every day:

- **From the drop-off point to the classroom door:** is it safe for small children, and do you have to cross a driveway?
- **The lobby and lifts:** who takes children up to class, and do they share the lift with other people in the building?
- **Stairs and emergency exits:** are they clearly signposted and kept clear?
- **Play areas on upper floors (if any):** railings, shade, flooring, and who supervises children while they play.

These questions are not meant to put the school on the spot. A well-prepared school will answer clearly and show you around in person.
