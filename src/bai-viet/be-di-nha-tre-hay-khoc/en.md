Every morning it is the same: at the classroom door, your child clings to you and cries. Many parents go to work with a heavy heart, wondering whether they started school too early. This article explains why children cry at nursery, how long it takes to settle, what you can do, and when to talk more with the teacher.

## Why does my child cry?

At nursery age, children are deeply attached to their parents and main carers. When they have to be apart from them in an unfamiliar place, they feel anxious and cry. This is a natural reaction that shows a healthy bond with the family, not a sign that your child is "naughty" or that the school is not good.

A few things make children cry more:

- **Everything is new:** new adults, new friends, a new room, a new routine.
- **They cannot yet put all their feelings into words:** crying is a child's way of saying "I'm worried", "I'm tired", "I miss Mum".
- **They are tired or hungry:** not enough sleep, little breakfast, just recovered from illness.
- **They sense their parents' worry:** children are very sensitive to the faces and voices of the people close to them.

## How long does it take to settle?

There is no single number for every child. Many children cry a lot in the first days, then less and less. Some settle and then start crying again after a holiday, after being ill or when there is a big change at home. What to look at is the trend: does your child cry for a shorter time, calm down faster after you leave, and seem happier at pick-up?

A good sign is when the teacher says your child calmed down after a few minutes, then ate, played and slept with the other children. Many children cry hard at goodbye but are happy in class a little later.

## What to do each morning

- **Keep goodbyes short, clear and the same every day.** One hug, one familiar goodbye, then hand your child to the teacher. Long goodbyes and coming back several times often make it harder to let go.
- **Do not slip away when your child is not looking.** It may make that morning easier, but your child will worry more on the following days because they do not know when you might disappear.
- **Say when you will be back using a moment your child understands:** "Mum will come after your afternoon snack", and arrive when you said.
- **Keep a calm, cheerful face.** When you are confident, your child gradually feels safe too.
- **Let your child bring a familiar item** if the school allows: a small towel, a little soft toy.

## What to do at home

- **Make sure your child sleeps enough and eats breakfast** before school. Tired children cry more easily.
- **Talk about school positively**: mention the teacher's name, friends' names, a food your child likes at school.
- **Read picture books about going to school** so your child understands what will happen each day.
- **Spend one-to-one time with your child after pick-up.** Fifteen minutes of play together, with no phone, helps your child "recharge" the bond.
- **Do not use school as a threat.** "If you're naughty, I'll leave you at school all day" makes children afraid of class.

## When to talk more with the teacher

Talk in more detail with the teacher if, after some time, your child still cries a lot, or shows changes such as refusing food, sleeping badly, being easily startled or not wanting to play even at home. The teacher is with your child all day, so they can tell you how your child is in class and work with you to help. If your child shows health signs such as fever or prolonged tiredness, take them to see a doctor.

## How are new children helped to settle at Casa?

Teachers work with parents to help children get used to the new environment and keep them updated on how their child is settling in during the first weeks. Teachers observe children's emotions and help them recognise and express their needs. Teaching assistants work with the class teacher to observe each child's needs, especially for children who are new or still settling in.

Parents follow their child's day through the school's communication app and talk directly with the teacher. The admissions process includes a trial day before enrolment, and the school supports families through the first weeks. The detailed steps are on the [Admissions](/en/admissions/) page.
