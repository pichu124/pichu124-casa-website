Many parents want their child to be independent, to listen and to follow shared routines, but also worry about pushing too early. In fact, independence is not a separate lesson. It grows from the small things a child is allowed to do for themselves every day. This article suggests tasks for each age, how you can help at home, and how a preschool class builds these habits with your child.

## What does independence mean at preschool age?

At 3–6, independence means a child can manage their own everyday tasks: eating, dressing, using the toilet, putting things away, getting ready for an activity. It also means daring to choose, daring to try, and knowing how to ask for help when needed.

Independence does not mean doing everything alone or doing it perfectly. A child who feeds themselves and spills is still learning independence. So is a child who asks the teacher to tie their shoelaces, because they know what they need and can say it.

When children do things for themselves, they feel useful and believe in their own abilities. That confidence carries over into bigger things, including starting Grade 1.

## Small tasks for each age

Every child grows at their own pace. The list below is a starting point, not milestones your child must reach.

**Around 3–4 years**
- Feeding themselves and holding a cup to drink.
- Taking off shoes and coat and putting them in the right place.
- Washing hands before eating and after using the toilet.
- Putting toys back in the basket after play.

**Around 4–5 years**
- Putting on simple clothes and shoes.
- Using the toilet on their own and remembering to wash hands.
- Clearing their own bowl and spoon after a meal.
- Choosing between two or three outfits or toys.

**Around 5–6 years**
- Getting their things ready for the next day and carrying their own backpack.
- Helping with small household jobs: watering plants, setting out chopsticks, folding towels.
- Finishing one task before moving on to the next.
- Asking for help or asking again when they do not understand.

## How can you help at home?

- **Give your child time.** Children are much slower than adults. Get up ten minutes earlier so your child can put on their own shirt, instead of dressing them to save time.
- **Keep things within reach.** Low coat hooks, a shoe rack at their height, a toy basket with a fixed place. Children can only tidy up when they know where things go and can reach them.
- **Break tasks into small steps.** Instead of "go and have a bath", say each step: take off your shirt, put it in the basket, go into the bathroom.
- **Praise effort, not just results.** "You did up two buttons by yourself" makes a child want to keep trying more than "you're so clever".
- **Don't redo it in front of them.** A blanket folded unevenly is fine. If you refold it straight away, your child learns that what they did does not count.
- **Keep the same routine.** When home and school work the same way, children get used to it faster. Ask the teacher what your child already does for themselves in class, then let them do it at home too.

## Independence and routine, not spoiling or forcing

Many parents feel caught between two extremes: too soft and the child relies on others, too strict and the child becomes afraid. The middle way is to set clear routines and help your child follow them with patience.

- **Say what will happen next.** "Play a little longer, then we'll put the toys away and have our meal." Children cooperate more easily when they are not surprised.
- **Offer choices within limits.** "Would you like the blue shirt or the red one?" rather than "what do you want to wear?". Your child gets to choose, and you keep the routine.
- **Keep your voice gentle but firm.** When your child does not want to do something yet, repeat the request calmly and do the first step together, without shouting.

## How do children practise independence at Casa?

At Casa, the programme for children aged 3–6, based on the Cambridge Early Years framework, has six areas of development, including personal, social and emotional development. Alongside the programme based on the Cambridge Early Years framework, the school uses Montessori methods and materials to support children’s learning.

At mealtimes, teachers stay with the children and help them learn to serve themselves and keep clean. Autonomy is one of Casa's five core values: every child is encouraged to do things for themselves, make their own decisions and believe in their own abilities.
