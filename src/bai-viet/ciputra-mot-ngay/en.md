At a full-day preschool, your child spends almost the whole day in class: eating, learning, playing and napping with the teachers and other children. For working parents, knowing exactly how your child's day goes brings peace of mind and helps you prepare at home. This article walks through the main parts of a full day, with questions to ask when choosing a school in the Ciputra area.

## Morning: arriving and starting the day

The morning starts when you bring your child to class. The teacher welcomes your child and notices whether they seem tired or unusual today. Then children have breakfast and begin the day's activities.

Ask: what is the earliest drop-off time, who greets children at the classroom door, and how do you tell the teacher if your child had a slight fever or ate little at home that morning?

## Learning time and play time

For preschool children, learning and play go hand in hand. In the morning, children take part in class activities, active play, stories, songs and early English. Each age has its own suitable activities.

Ask: how long do children have active play each day, and where; where do they play when it rains; and what does a specific learning activity look like?

## Meals

A full day usually includes several meals: breakfast, lunch, a snack and an afternoon meal. A regular, balanced, age-appropriate menu keeps children healthy and happy through a full day at school.

Ask: how many meals do children have each day, who cooks, where is the kitchen, how does the menu change, and how does the school handle allergies or fussy eating?

## Nap time

After lunch, children nap. Sleep gives them energy for the afternoon. New children may take a few days to get used to sleeping at school.

Ask: where do children sleep, does each child have their own place to lie down, and what does the teacher do when a child struggles to sleep or wakes early? Tell the teacher about your child's sleep habits at home so they can settle your child the right way.

## Afternoon and pick-up

In the afternoon, children wake up, have a snack and take part in calmer activities than in the morning. At the end of the day, the teacher gets children ready to go home and has a short chat with parents about the day.

Ask: what is the latest pick-up time, is late care available, who is allowed to pick your child up, and how will you know how your child ate and slept during the day?

## Helping your child get used to a full day

A day at school is much longer than a day at home, so your child may be tired in the first few weeks. You can prepare in advance:

- **Bring meal and nap times at home closer to school times** about two to four weeks before your child starts.
- **Let your child practise small tasks:** washing hands, feeding themselves, putting things away, telling the teacher when they need the toilet.
- **Put your child to bed early** in the first weeks, so they have energy for a long day.
- **Ask your child about their day each evening** with specific questions: "What did you eat today?", "Who did you play with?", rather than "Did you have fun at school?".

## A day at Casa

At Casa's current campuses, children attend from 7:30 to 17:30, Monday to Friday. They have 4–5 meals a day depending on the campus, cooked by the school's own one-way kitchen, including cakes and snacks. Parents who need a later pick-up can register for late care until 18:30 (fees apply). Children keep learning in summer, with just 1 week off according to the school calendar.
