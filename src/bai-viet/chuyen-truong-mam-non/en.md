Changing schools in the middle of the school year is not an easy decision for any family: your child has just got used to the teacher and friends, and now has to start again. But there are times when a change is the sensible choice for the whole family. This article helps you weigh when to change, what to ask the new school, and how to prepare so your child settles into the new class gently.

## When should you consider changing schools?

Every family has its own reasons. Some common situations:

- **The family moves house or changes workplace**, and the school run becomes too long, so your child gets up early and comes home late every day.
- **The hours no longer fit** your work schedule, for example you need late care or school during the summer.
- **Your child's needs change**: they reach kindergarten age, you want them to follow a different programme or track, or you want them to prepare for Grade 1 in a different way.
- **Your child has been unhappy at school for a long time**, even though the family has talked with the teacher and the school many times.

If the reason is that your child has not settled yet, talk with the teacher in detail first. Many difficulties, such as crying at goodbye or not yet being used to meals and naps, can improve when the family and the teacher work together. Changing too soon can mean your child has to settle all over again.

## What to ask the new school before deciding

1. **Is there a place** for your child's age at a campus near home?
2. **Does the school offer a trial** before enrolling?
3. **How does the school help new children settle**, especially mid-year when the other children already know each other?
4. **What does a day look like** at the new school: learning time, meals, nap time, pick-up?
5. **What documents are needed** to enrol, and how are fees calculated when starting mid-year?

The article [8 questions to ask on a preschool visit](/en/news/8-questions-to-ask-on-a-preschool-visit/) has more detailed questions for a visit.

## Preparing your child before the move

- **Take your child to see the new school** before the first day, so they can see the classroom, toys and play area.
- **Explain simply and positively**: "You'll be in a new class, with a new teacher and new friends." Avoid speaking badly of the old school in front of your child.
- **Let your child say goodbye** to the teachers and friends at the old school if possible. A goodbye helps them close the old chapter more gently.
- **Keep home routines the same** in the first weeks: sleep times, meal times, familiar things.
- **Tell the new teacher** about your child's habits, likes, fears, and what they learned at the old school.

## The first weeks in the new class

A child who joins mid-year may need some time to fit into a group of friends who already know each other well. They may cry again, eat less or be more tired for a few days. Pick your child up on time, ask specific questions about their day, and keep in regular touch with the teacher. If your child cries a lot at goodbye, the article [Crying at nursery drop-off: why it happens and what to do](/en/news/crying-at-nursery-drop-off/) has more suggestions.

## Getting to know Casa

Casa welcomes children from 15 months to 6 years, according to the programme and admission conditions at each campus. The school accepts children in the middle of the school year when a class has places. All three campuses, Kim Mã, Chùa Láng and Mỹ Đình, welcome parents to visit. Children attend from 7:30 to 17:30, Monday to Friday, with late care until 18:30 (fees apply), and school continues through the summer.

Admissions follow five steps: the school receives your details, gives advice and books a visit, your family visits for about 30–45 minutes, your child can have a trial if you wish, then enrols, then the school supports your family through the first weeks. To find out which campus has a place for your child's age, please contact the school or register on the [Admissions](/en/admissions/) page. Registrations sent during office hours receive a call back the same working day.
