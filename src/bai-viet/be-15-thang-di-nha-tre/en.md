Around 15 months is when many families start thinking about nursery. There is no single "right" day for every child: each one grows at their own pace. This article helps you recognise the signs that your child is ready, and prepare at home so the first week in class is gentler for the whole family.

## Five signs your child is ready

Your child does not need to show all five signs. If they show most of them, you can feel confident about starting to look at nurseries.

1. **They walk more steadily and love to explore.** Your child walks and climbs on their own and is curious about the things around them. Nursery classes include plenty of movement, so a child who likes to get around will enjoy it more.
2. **They can eat textured food and are used to set mealtimes.** Your child eats thick porridge, soft rice and finely chopped vegetables, and is used to eating at fixed times of day.
3. **Their sleep is fairly regular.** Your child naps at roughly the same times each day. A regular sleep pattern makes it easier to fit into the class routine.
4. **They can play for a while without you beside them.** Your child plays with grandparents, relatives or children of the same age for a short time without too much worry.
5. **They are starting to "talk" in their own way.** Your child points, nods or shakes their head, and says a few single words to show what they want. They do not need to say much yet, only to have a way to express their needs.

If your child does not show these signs yet, there is no need to worry. They may need a few more months, or some gradual practice at home as suggested below.

## What to prepare at home

Start about two to four weeks before your child begins, a little each day.

- **Adjust the daily routine gradually.** Bring your child's wake-up, meal and nap times closer to the school's schedule. At Casa, children attend from 7:30 to 17:30, Monday to Friday.
- **Let your child do a few small things alone.** Holding a spoon to eat, holding a cup to drink, putting toys back in the basket. It does not matter if they are clumsy at first; what matters is getting used to doing things themselves.
- **Practise short separations.** Leave your child with a relative for a short time, then increase it gradually. When you leave, say goodbye clearly and say when you will be back; do not slip away when your child is not looking.
- **Talk to your child about school.** Read picture books about going to school and talk cheerfully about the teacher and the other children. Your child will not understand everything, but will feel your confidence.
- **Prepare familiar things.** Easy-to-change clothes, a towel, your child's own water bottle, and a familiar comfort item if the school allows it.

## The first week in class

The first week is when your child gets to know new people, a new place and a new routine. Crying when saying goodbye is normal at this age.

A few things help children settle more easily:

- **Keep goodbyes short and consistent.** One hug and the same goodbye each morning, then hand your child to the teacher. Long goodbyes often make it harder to let go.
- **Pick up at the time you promised.** When you arrive when you said you would, your child gradually learns that you always come back.
- **Talk with the teacher every day.** Ask how your child ate and slept, who they played with and whether anything was unusual. Tell the teacher about your child's habits at home so they can comfort your child in the right way.
- **Keep home life steady.** In the first weeks your child may be tired and need to sleep earlier. Avoid big changes at home during this time.

At Casa, teachers work with parents to help children get used to the new environment and keep them updated on how their child is settling in during the first weeks.

## Nursery classes at Casa

Casa welcomes children from 15 months, according to the programme and admission conditions at each campus. Nursery classes are offered at all three campuses: Kim Mã, Chùa Láng and Mỹ Đình.

Nursery groups for 15–36 months are organised along Montessori principles, focusing on independence, the senses, movement, language and practical life skills. Children get used to English naturally through songs, games, stories and everyday conversation. Each day they have 4 meals cooked by the school's own one-way kitchen.

Read more about nursery classes on the [Nursery, 15–36 months](/en/programmes/nursery/) page.

## When you visit a nursery

Bring your child along when you visit, to see how they respond to the class and the teacher. Take questions about the daily schedule, meals, naps and how the school helps new children settle in. The article [8 questions to ask on a preschool visit](/en/news/8-questions-to-ask-on-a-preschool-visit/) has a ready-made list you can use.

How to book a visit and a trial day at Casa is explained on the [Admissions](/en/admissions/) page.
