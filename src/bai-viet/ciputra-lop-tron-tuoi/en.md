When looking for a nursery, many parents come across the term "mixed-age class" and wonder: will a young child lose out by learning with older children, and can the teacher keep up with each child? This article explains what a mixed-age class is, how children under 3 learn in it, and the questions worth asking when you look into one.

## What is a mixed-age class?

A mixed-age class is a class where children of different ages learn together in one room, instead of each class having just one age group. It is a familiar way of organising classes in education in the spirit of Montessori.

In the class, materials are set out on low shelves within children's reach. A child chooses an activity they like, works on it for as long as suits them, then puts it back. The teacher observes each child and introduces new activities when the child is ready, rather than the whole class doing the same thing at the same time.

## What do younger children gain?

- **Learning by watching older children.** Young children love to imitate. Seeing older children wash their hands, put things away and feed themselves, they want to do the same.
- **Hearing more language.** Older children speak in longer sentences, so younger ones hear and practise talking in very natural situations.
- **Going at their own pace.** Because the whole class is not doing the same thing, younger children are not pushed to keep up with older ones.

## What do older children gain?

- **Practising helping and waiting.** Older children practise giving way, waiting for younger friends and showing them how to do things. These are early steps towards caring and patience.
- **Revisiting what they know.** When they show a younger child, older children repeat something they already know, which helps them remember it and feel more confident.
- **Being the "big one".** Feeling grown-up and useful helps older children feel more confident in class.

## What do parents often worry about?

**Will my young child be overwhelmed by older ones?** This is worth asking the school. A good mixed-age class has enough materials for each age, a quiet area for the youngest, and teachers who notice when a younger child needs protecting.

**Will older children be held back?** In a mixed-age class, each child works on activities that suit them. Older children are introduced to harder activities when they are ready, and do not have to wait for younger ones.

**Can the teacher keep up with each child?** Ask how many adults are in the class, how the teacher tracks each child's progress, and how the teacher keeps parents informed.

## What to ask when looking at a mixed-age class

1. What age range does the class cover, in months?
2. Which adults are in the class, and what does each one do?
3. What activities make up the day, and when are meals and naps?
4. Do the youngest children have a suitable area and materials of their own?
5. How does the teacher track and share your child's progress?

The article [8 questions to ask on a preschool visit](/en/news/8-questions-to-ask-on-a-preschool-visit/) has more general questions for a visit.

## How can you help at home?

Mixed-age classes encourage children to do things and make choices themselves. You can keep the same spirit at home:

- **Keep your child's things within reach.** A low shelf for toys, a low hook for their jacket, a cup they can fetch themselves.
- **Let your child choose between a few options.** "Do you want the blue shirt or the red one?" helps them practise deciding without too many choices.
- **Wait for your child to finish.** When they are feeding themselves or putting on shoes, try not to do it for them, even if they are slow.
- **Let your child play with siblings and children of other ages.** Playtime with cousins or neighbours is also a chance to learn by watching and imitating.
