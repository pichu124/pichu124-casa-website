Many parents want their child to start English early, but wonder: is my child too young and will they get confused, do they need to learn letters and vocabulary, and how should we choose a school? This article explains how preschool children get to know English, what to do at home, and what to ask a school so you choose well.

## How do preschool children get to know English?

At preschool age, children learn language mainly by listening, imitating and using it in real situations, just as they learn Vietnamese. So for young children, "learning English" does not mean sitting down to learn letters or copy new words. It means hearing songs and stories, playing games, greeting people and talking about the things around them in English, regularly, every day.

The goal at this age is simply for children to get used to the sounds of English, understand simple sentences and enjoy using it. A child may understand for quite a long time before starting to speak. That is normal.

## Things to avoid

- **Pushing your child to speak or testing them all the time.** "Say something in English for the teacher" can make a child shy. Let them speak when they feel comfortable.
- **Memorising isolated words.** A word list that is not tied to any situation is often quickly forgotten. Children remember better when words come with an activity: "wash your hands" while washing hands, "apple" while eating an apple.
- **Letting screens replace people.** Videos can be part of it, but children learn language better when a real person talks with them and responds.
- **Comparing your child with others.** Every child has their own pace. Some speak early; some listen for a long time before speaking.

## How can you help at home?

You do not need to speak English well to help your child:

- **Sing and listen to English songs together**, especially ones with actions.
- **Read bilingual picture books** before bed. Point to the pictures and name the objects.
- **Use a few familiar phrases every day**: "Good morning", "Thank you", "Let's go", "All done".
- **Praise effort and avoid harsh corrections.** When your child makes a mistake, simply repeating the correct phrase naturally is enough.
- **Keep your home language strong.** Children still need a strong mother tongue to express their feelings and thoughts.

## What to ask when choosing a school

Programme and track names often sound the same from one school to the next, so it pays to ask specifically:

1. Through which activities during the day do children get to know English?
2. Who in the class speaks English, and is the foreign teacher in the class or teaching set lessons?
3. How does the school keep parents informed about their child's progress in English?
4. Do children use English in daily routines, or only in English lessons?

## English at Casa

- **Nursery, 15–36 months:** children get used to English naturally through songs, games, stories and everyday conversation.
- **Kindergarten, 3–6 years:** children follow a programme based on the Cambridge Early Years framework for children aged 3–6. Foreign teachers take part in English activities according to each track's model. Through conversation, stories, games and exploration, children have the chance to hear, understand and use English in situations suited to their age.

Classes for ages 3–6 have two tracks:

| | International track | Bilingual track |
| --- | --- | --- |
| In the class | 1 homeroom teacher, 1 teaching assistant (preferably a strong English speaker), 1 foreign teacher | 1 homeroom teacher, 2 teaching assistants (at least 1 strong English speaker) |
| Foreign teacher | 1, in the class | Teaches set lessons |
| Campuses | Chùa Láng, Mỹ Đình | Kim Mã |
