The last year of kindergarten is often the year parents worry about most: can my child read yet, can they write, will they keep up with the others in Grade 1? In fact, what helps a child start Grade 1 well is much broader than letters and numbers. This article explains what a pre-primary year should include, and what you can do at home to support your child.

## What changes when your child starts Grade 1?

In kindergarten, children learn mainly through play, move around a lot and have a teacher beside them in most activities. In Grade 1, they sit through lessons, look after their own things, listen to instructions given to the whole class and handle more on their own during the day.

So getting ready for Grade 1 does not mean learning the Grade 1 curriculum early. What your child needs is to be ready for a new way of learning: able to concentrate, to listen, to look after themselves and to want to find things out.

## Five skill areas for the pre-primary year

**1. Language and early literacy.** Your child retells a story in order, answers questions in full sentences, and listens to and tells sounds apart. They are familiar with books, understand that writing carries meaning, hold a pencil correctly and trace and draw basic strokes. This is the foundation that makes learning to read and write in Grade 1 easier.

**2. Mathematical thinking.** Your child counts, compares more and fewer, adds and takes away with small numbers, and recognises shapes and simple patterns. What matters is understanding numbers in real situations, not just reciting them.

**3. Independence and self-care.** Your child dresses, uses the toilet and eats on their own, and puts away and finds their own things. A child who can manage these will feel much more secure in a new environment.

**4. Concentration and following instructions.** Your child can stay with one task for a reasonable time, listens to the whole instruction before starting, and finishes one task before moving on to the next.

**5. Emotions and friendships.** Your child can say whether they feel happy, sad or worried; can wait their turn, share and ask for help when needed; and can make new friends. These skills help children settle in when the class, the teachers and the other children all change at once.

## Pre-primary at Casa

At Casa, children aged 3–6 follow a programme based on the Cambridge Early Years framework. The framework is divided into three years, Early Years 1, 2 and 3, with six areas of development: communication, language and literacy; creative expression; mathematics; personal, social and emotional development; physical development; and understanding the world.

The final year is the pre-primary year and follows Early Years 3. Children use what they have learned in real situations, for example using counting to compare, add and take away and solve small problems, while building extra skills to prepare for Grade 1. All three campuses, Kim Mã, Chùa Láng and Mỹ Đình, teach the pre-primary programme. Chùa Láng and Mỹ Đình have a separate pre-primary class. At Kim Mã, children aged 3–6 share one class; in the afternoons, 5–6-year-olds study the pre-primary programme separately.

See the three kindergarten years on the [Kindergarten, 3–6 years](/en/programmes/kindergarten/#tien-tieu-hoc) page. The article [What is Cambridge Early Years?](/en/news/what-is-cambridge-early-years/) explains the framework in more detail.

## How can you help at home?

- **Read with your child every day.** Fifteen minutes each evening is enough. Ask about the characters, guess what happens next, and let your child retell the story in their own words.
- **Bring maths into daily life.** Count chopsticks when setting the table, compare which cup has more water, share a cake among the family.
- **Give your child small jobs of their own.** Packing their bag, putting away their shoes, watering the plants. A child who is responsible for small tasks gets used to looking after themselves in Grade 1.
- **Build a steady routine.** Early to bed, up on time, breakfast before school. A regular routine helps your child start the new school year without being tired.
- **Talk about Grade 1 positively.** Tell your child about the interesting things at their new school, and avoid warnings like "in Grade 1 you have to behave and study". A child who is excited settles in more easily than a child who is afraid.

## Things not to worry too much about

It is normal for a child not to read fluently or write neatly by the end of kindergarten: Grade 1 teaches letters from the beginning. Learning too much in advance can make a child bored in class, or lead to habits in holding a pencil or forming letters that are not quite right.

What deserves more attention is whether your child enjoys going to school, feels confident to ask when they do not understand, and can play with other children. If you notice your child finding something difficult, talk to the teacher so you can help together during the last year of kindergarten.
