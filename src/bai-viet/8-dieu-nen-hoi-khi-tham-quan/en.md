A preschool visit usually lasts only 30–45 minutes. In that time you are looking at classrooms, listening to the introduction and watching whether your child feels at ease, so it is easy to forget the important questions. Preparing them in advance helps you compare schools on the same points and miss nothing.

## 1. What does a child's day look like?

Ask for the actual schedule from arrival to home time: lessons, play, meals and the afternoon nap. A clear, steady routine helps young children settle. Ask too about the latest pick-up time and whether the school offers late care.

For example, at Casa children attend from 7:30 to 17:30, Monday to Friday; parents who need to pick up later can register for late care until 18:30 (charged).

## 2. Who is in each class?

Ask how many adults are in the class and what each one does: homeroom teacher, teaching assistants, English teacher. If the school has more than one track, ask how they differ rather than relying on the names. A comparison of Casa's two tracks is on the [Programmes](/en/programmes/) page.

## 3. What do children eat, and who cooks?

Ask to see the weekly menu, how many meals children have a day, where the kitchen is and who cooks. If your child has allergies or is a fussy eater, ask how the school handles this at everyday meals.

At Casa, children have 4–5 meals a day depending on the campus (4 at Kim Mã; Chùa Láng and Mỹ Đình add fresh milk at 16:00), from a menu suited to preschool age. The school's one-way kitchen cooks everything itself, including cakes and snacks.

## 4. Which programme do children follow, and through what activities?

Ask the name of the programme the school follows and ask to see something concrete: a learning corner, a set of materials, a lesson in progress. A good programme for young children shows in how teachers run activities, not only in its name.

If a school says it follows the Cambridge Early Years framework, the article [What is Cambridge Early Years?](/en/news/what-is-cambridge-early-years/) explains what that programme includes, so you can ask the right questions.

## 5. How are safety and hygiene handled?

Ask who may pick up your child, how the school controls who comes in, and what happens if a child falls ill or gets hurt during the day. As you walk around, look for yourself: toilets, stairs, furniture corners, how clean the toys are.

## 6. How do new children settle in?

The first week is hard for children and parents alike. Ask whether there is a settling-in schedule, whether parents can stay with their child for the first days, and how the teacher will keep you updated.

At Casa, teachers work with parents to help children get used to their new surroundings and keep them updated on how their child is settling in.

## 7. How does the school keep in touch with families?

Ask how you will know how your child ate, slept and learned each day, and how often you can talk one to one with the teacher. Regular contact helps you notice early if something is not right.

## 8. What do the fees include, and what is charged separately?

Do not ask only for the total. Ask what the fee schedule covers: tuition, meals, late care, extracurricular activities, summer programme, enrolment fee. Two schools with similar totals can differ a lot item by item.

At Casa, the school sends the fee schedule after talking with the family about the child's age, campus and track. Late care, extracurricular activities and the summer programme are by registration and charged separately.

## After the visit

Write down your impressions as soon as you leave, while they are fresh. Beyond the school's answers, think back to how the teachers spoke to the children, the atmosphere in the class and whether your child seemed curious and comfortable. Comparing schools on these same 8 questions makes the decision much easier.
